Privacy Policy

Saeloria Demo · Last updated 24 November 2026

Saeloria Demo is a private, invite-only preview of Saeloria, a social network for women. I’m Maksym Maksymenko, a developer in Bucharest, Romania, and I run it on my own. Saeloria isn’t a company yet, so I’m the one responsible for your data. The demo is for invited testers and investors, not a public service, and it is for adults only: you have to be 18 or over, and have an invite code, to create an account.

This policy says what the app collects, why, who can see it, who else handles it, how long it is kept, and how to have it deleted. It also says what I keep about testers outside the app. For any question about it, or to ask for your data or its deletion, write to me at maksimenko.maksim.2310@gmail.com.

What I collect

You give me most of it directly. The app records the rest as you use it.

Your account

  • Your email address and password. The password is stored only as a hash, so nobody can read it.
  • The invite code you used, which decides whether yours is a tester or an investor account. Its record also holds the name I made it out to (a first name and last initial on a tester code) and, on a tester code, how you were invited, such as through LinkedIn, an introduction, a student club or a friend. I use that only to count sign-ups by how people were invited.
  • Your name and date of birth, and, if you add them, a profile photo, a short bio and your identity pathway.
  • Whether you finished the simulated verification, and when.
  • Whether your new posts and comments start as Anonymous.

The verification steps only show a live camera viewfinder. The app never takes, saves or sends a picture of your ID or your face.

What you post and do

  • Your posts, with their text and photos, and when you edit, archive or delete them.
  • Your comments and replies, and their edits.
  • Your likes on posts and comments, and the posts you save.
  • The members you block, and whether you blocked them from a profile, a post or a comment.
  • Reports you file about a profile, post or comment: the reason you pick and the note you write. If you tell me that a sign-in on another phone wasn’t you, that goes in as a report too.
  • If an investigation in the demo blocks your account: the explanation and documents you send back through the evidence form.
  • On an investor account: the leaks you capture and check in the Leak Console (the result of the check, not the picture), and the investigations you open and close.

Post and profile photos are re-encoded on your phone before they are uploaded, which drops details such as where a photo was taken. Evidence documents are uploaded as they are.

Your sign-ins

Each time you sign in, the app records:

  • a random id it made for this copy of the app (not your phone’s own identifier),
  • your phone’s model and operating system version,
  • the country your internet connection is in, which my database provider’s network works out from your IP address. I store the country, not the address,
  • when you signed in, and which sign-in ended which, since only one phone can be signed in at a time.

While you’re signed in, the app also checks with the server that the demo is still open for your account, sending whether the phone runs iOS or Android and the app’s build number. That check isn’t stored.

Watermark records

When the app shows you a post’s photo, it records which post it was and when. That record is what lets a leaked copy of the photo be traced back to the account it was shown to (see Watermarks below).

How you use the demo

To learn how the demo is used, the app records events while you’re signed in: when you open and leave the app and how long you stayed, each screen you open, and what you do, such as posting, liking, saving, commenting, reporting, blocking, the guided tour’s steps, the My generation switch, attempts to take a screenshot and, on a tester account, your answer to the question after the ID scan (Yes, Not sure or No). An event carries ids (which post you liked, for example), yes-or-no answers, choices from a fixed list and counts. It never carries the text of a post, comment or report, a photo, or a link to one.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Sentry: what went wrong in the app’s code, your phone’s model and operating system, the app’s version, and, if you’re signed in, your account’s id and whether it is a tester or investor account. A crash before you’ve signed in sends a report with no account in it. Nothing is sent for errors the app survives, and a report never includes a screenshot, the screens you visited, or any post, comment or photo.

Kept on your phone only

Your sign-in (in the phone’s secure storage), the app’s random id, your guided-tour progress, whether the demo was still open the last time the app checked, and what the watermark needs on this phone stay on the phone, and are removed when you delete the app. A photo you pick for the Leak Console’s check is read on the phone and never uploaded.

Outside the app

If you’re a tester, I also keep a few things outside the app:

  • A list of the testers I’ve invited: your first name and last initial, how you were invited, and how far you’ve got in the test.
  • If you answer my questions in writing or agree to a short call, my notes of what you said. Calls are never recorded. The notes carry a tester number instead of your name, and only the list above links the number to you.
  • Our messages on LinkedIn or by email, which stay in those services under their own policies.

I use them to run the test and to learn whether women would verify their ID for an app like this. Investors and possible co-founders get only totals and findings that name no one. Answering my questions and taking a call are both up to you. By 31 January 2027 I’ll delete the list and anything else I keep that names you, apart from our messages. Until then you can ask me to delete my notes of your answers or call; after that they stay, with no way to tell who said what.

Why I use it

  • To run the app: your account, what other members see of you, and the one-phone-at-a-time sign-in.
  • To check you are 18 or over, and for the feed’s My generation filter. Both use your date of birth; no one sees your age.
  • To show how a leaked copy is traced: the watermarks and their records.
  • To run the demo’s moderation: reports, investigations and evidence.
  • To understand how the demo is used, and to fix crashes.

I use your data to provide the app you asked for, and because you agreed to this policy. I don’t sell it, show ads, or use it to track you across other apps or websites, so a Do Not Track setting has nothing to turn off.

Who can see what

  • Other members see your profile photo, name and bio, and your posts and comments that aren’t anonymous. They never see your email, date of birth, age or identity pathway. A post’s like count may show; who liked or saved it doesn’t.
  • Anonymous hides your name from other members, the author of a post you comment on included. The app still stores who wrote every anonymous post and comment.
  • Who you block is yours alone. The member isn’t told, no one else sees it, and blocking from an anonymous post or comment never tells you who wrote it.
  • Investor accounts belong to the investors and possible co-founders I’ve invited to look at Saeloria, and to Apple’s reviewers. They have an admin view for the demo’s moderation. It shows reports and who filed them, the watermark records (which account was shown which post, and when), leak investigations, the evidence sent in, and posts that were archived or deleted, until a deleted post is erased.
  • I can see everything stored, the usage events included, to run the demo.

Watermarks

Every photo and screen the app shows you carries marks tied to your account, some you can see and some you can’t, so a leaked copy can be traced back to it. These marks, and the blocking of screenshots and screen recording, run on your phone. They demonstrate how Saeloria would discourage leaks. They are not a security guarantee, and someone determined can get around them.

Photos are stored without the marks, in private storage. The app loads each one through a link that only a signed-in member can get and that stops working after an hour, and it never shows those links. Evidence documents are kept in private storage too.

Who else handles your data

Two service providers handle data for me:

  • Supabase keeps the database, the sign-ins, and uploaded photos and documents, on servers in Northern California, in the United States. Its own logs record the IP address of requests and sign-ins for a short time, to run and secure its service.
  • Sentry receives crash reports, on servers in the United States, and is set not to store IP addresses. It keeps a report for 30 days.

Each handles the data only to provide its service to me, under data-protection terms that protect it at least as well as this policy does.

Fixes to the app reach your phone through Expo. Each time the app starts, it asks Expo’s servers whether there is an update. It sends your phone’s operating system, the app’s version and a random number that identifies this installation of the app, which isn’t tied to your account. Expo’s servers also see your phone’s IP address, as any server it connects to does. Expo may process this in the United States and handles it under its own privacy policy.

I’m in the EU, so sending data to the United States needs a legal safeguard. Sentry and Expo are certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, and Supabase’s terms cover the data it holds with the EU’s standard contractual clauses.

I use an AI assistant, Anthropic’s Claude, to help run the test. It reads the usage records, the list of testers and my notes, to count the results and write them up. Anthropic is in the United States and handles this under its own privacy policy. I’ve turned off its use of my conversations to train its models.

If you installed the demo through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple handles its own data under its own privacy policy. TestFlight shows me how many times you opened the demo, the day you installed it, which build you have and how often it crashed. If you joined through a public link, it doesn’t show me your name or email address. Feedback you send through TestFlight does include your email address.

How long I keep it

  • Everything you add stays until you delete it or your account, or ask me to delete it.
  • A post you delete disappears at once, along with its likes and comments, and is erased 30 days later. Until then, investor accounts can still see it. The photo file of a deleted post, and a photo you replace when editing a post, stay in storage, out of the app’s reach, until you delete your account or ask me to remove them.
  • A comment you delete is erased at once. If someone replied to it, it stays as “deleted comment” above the reply, with no name and no text.
  • My list of testers, and anything else I keep outside the app that names you, until 31 January 2027 at the latest (see Outside the app).
  • Crash reports are kept by Sentry for 30 days.
  • Expo keeps what an update check sends for as long as its own policy finds reasonably necessary, and gives no fixed period.

Deleting your account

Go to Settings → Account → Delete account and confirm with your password. It happens at once. If your phone was signed out because your account signed in on another one, sign in again first. If the demo has ended for your account, the app no longer opens Settings, so write to me and I’ll delete the account for you.

It deletes your profile and photo, your posts and their photos, your comments, likes and saves, the members you blocked, the reports you filed, the evidence you sent, your sign-ins, your usage events, the invite code you used, and the account itself. On an investor account it also deletes the leaks you captured, with their investigations. A comment someone replied to stays as “deleted comment” above the reply, with no name and no text. Coming back takes a new invite code.

What stays: reports other members filed about you, your posts or your comments, which no longer show your account; and crash reports already in Sentry, which carry your account’s id until Sentry deletes them after 30 days. Update checks at Expo aren’t tied to your account, so deleting it doesn’t reach them.

Your rights

Write to me at maksimenko.maksim.2310@gmail.com to ask for a copy of what I hold about you, to correct it, to delete it or your account, to limit or object to how I use it, or to get it in a file you can take elsewhere. I’ll answer within 30 days. If you think I’ve handled your data wrongly, you can also complain to Romania’s data-protection authority, ANSPDCP.

Your consent

You agree to this policy when you tick the box as you create an account, and each time you sign in. You can withdraw that at any time by deleting your account, which stops all collection. Signing out stops everything but crash reports on that phone.

Changes

When this policy changes, I’ll update this page and the date at the top. The app always links to the current version.
