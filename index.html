Privacy Policy

Saeloria Demo · Last updated 21 October 2026

Saeloria Demo is a private, invite-only preview of Saeloria, a social network for women. It is run by the Saeloria team (“we”). It is a demonstration for invited testers and investors, not a public service, and it is for adults only: you have to be 18 or over, and have an invite code, to create an account.

This policy says what the app collects, why, who can see it, who else handles it, how long it is kept, and how to have it deleted. For any question about it, or to ask for your data or its deletion, write to maksimenko.maksim.2310@gmail.com.

What we collect

You give us most of it directly. The app records the rest as you use it.

Your account

  • Your email address and password. The password is stored only as a hash, so nobody can read it.
  • The invite code you used. It decides whether yours is a tester or an investor account.
  • Your name and date of birth, and, if you add them, a profile photo, a short bio and your identity pathway.
  • Whether you finished the simulated verification, and when.
  • Whether your new posts and comments start as Anonymous.

The verification steps only show a live camera viewfinder. The app never takes, saves or sends a picture of your ID or your face.

What you post and do

  • Your posts, with their text and photos, and when you edit, archive or delete them.
  • Your comments and replies, and their edits.
  • Your likes on posts and comments, and the posts you save.
  • Reports you file about a profile, post or comment: the reason you pick and the note you write. If you tell us that a sign-in on another phone wasn’t you, that goes in as a report too.
  • If an investigation in the demo blocks your account: the explanation and documents you send back through the evidence form.
  • On an investor account: the leaks you capture and check in the Leak Console (the result of the check, not the picture), and the investigations you open and close.

Post and profile photos are re-encoded on your phone before they are uploaded, which drops details such as where a photo was taken. Evidence documents are uploaded as they are.

Your sign-ins

Each time you sign in, the app records:

  • a random id it made for this copy of the app (not your phone’s own identifier),
  • your phone’s model and operating system version,
  • the country your internet connection is in, which our database provider’s network works out from your IP address. We store the country, not the address,
  • when you signed in, and which sign-in ended which, since only one phone can be signed in at a time.

While you’re signed in, the app also checks with our server that the demo is still open for your account, sending whether the phone runs iOS or Android and the app’s build number. That check isn’t stored.

Watermark records

When the app shows you a post’s photo, it records which post it was and when. That record is what lets a leaked copy of the photo be traced back to the account it was shown to (see Watermarks below).

How you use the demo

To learn how the demo is used, the app records events while you’re signed in: when you open and leave the app and how long you stayed, each screen you open, and what you do, such as posting, liking, saving, commenting, reporting, the guided tour’s steps, the My generation preview, and attempts to take a screenshot. An event carries ids (which post you liked, for example), yes-or-no answers and counts. It never carries the text of a post, comment or report, a photo, or a link to one.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Sentry: what went wrong in the app’s code, your phone’s model and operating system, the app’s version, and, if you’re signed in, your account’s id and whether it is a tester or investor account. A crash before you’ve signed in sends a report with no account in it. Nothing is sent for errors the app survives, and a report never includes a screenshot, the screens you visited, or any post, comment or photo.

Kept on your phone only

Your sign-in (in the phone’s secure storage), the app’s random id, your guided-tour progress, whether the demo was still open the last time the app checked, and what the watermark needs on this phone stay on the phone, and are removed when you delete the app. A photo you pick for the Leak Console’s check is read on the phone and never uploaded.

Why we use it

  • To run the app: your account, what other members see of you, and the one-phone-at-a-time sign-in.
  • To check you are 18 or over, and for the feed’s My generation filter. Both use your date of birth; no one sees your age.
  • To show how a leaked copy is traced: the watermarks and their records.
  • To run the demo’s moderation: reports, investigations and evidence.
  • To understand how the demo is used, and to fix crashes.

We use your data to provide the app you asked for, and because you agreed to this policy. We don’t sell it, show ads, or use it to track you across other apps or websites.

Who can see what

  • Other members see your profile photo, name and bio, and your posts and comments that aren’t anonymous. They never see your email, date of birth, age or identity pathway. A post’s like count may show; who liked or saved it doesn’t.
  • Anonymous hides your name from other members, the author of a post you comment on included. Saeloria still stores who wrote every anonymous post and comment.
  • Investor accounts have an admin view for the demo’s moderation. It shows reports, the watermark records (which account was shown which post, and when), leak investigations, the evidence sent in, and posts that were archived or deleted, until a deleted post is erased.
  • The Saeloria team can see everything stored, the usage events included, to run the demo.

Watermarks

Every photo and screen the app shows you carries marks tied to your account, some you can see and some you can’t, so a leaked copy can be traced back to it. These marks, and the blocking of screenshots and screen recording, run on your phone. They demonstrate how Saeloria would discourage leaks. They are not a security guarantee, and someone determined can get around them.

Photos are stored without the marks, in storage that gives a photo to anyone who has its exact link. The app never shows those links. Evidence documents are kept in private storage.

Who else handles your data

Two service providers handle data for us:

  • Supabase keeps the database, the sign-ins, and uploaded photos and documents, on servers in Frankfurt, Germany. Its own logs record the IP address of requests and sign-ins for a short time, to run and secure its service.
  • Sentry receives crash reports, on servers in the United States, and is set not to store IP addresses. It keeps a report for 30 days.

Each handles the data only to provide its service to us, under data-protection terms that protect it at least as well as this policy does. The service you install the demo through, such as Apple’s TestFlight, handles its own data under its own privacy policy.

How long we keep it

  • Everything you add stays until you delete it or your account, or ask us to delete it.
  • A post you delete disappears at once, along with its likes and comments, and is erased 30 days later. Until then, investor accounts can still see it. The photo file of a deleted post, and a photo you replace when editing a post, stay in storage, out of the app’s reach, until you delete your account or ask us to remove them.
  • A comment you delete is erased at once. If someone replied to it, it stays as “deleted comment” above the reply, with no name and no text.
  • Crash reports are kept by Sentry for 30 days.

Deleting your account

Go to Settings → Account → Delete account and confirm with your password. It happens at once. If your phone was signed out because your account signed in on another one, sign in again first.

It deletes your profile and photo, your posts and their photos, your comments, likes and saves, the reports you filed, the evidence you sent, your sign-ins, your usage events, the invite code you used, and the account itself. On an investor account it also deletes the leaks you captured, with their investigations. A comment someone replied to stays as “deleted comment” above the reply, with no name and no text. Coming back takes a new invite code.

What stays: reports other members filed about you, your posts or your comments, which no longer show your account; and crash reports already in Sentry, which carry your account’s id until Sentry deletes them after 30 days.

Your rights

Write to maksimenko.maksim.2310@gmail.com to ask for a copy of what we hold about you, to correct it, or to delete it or your account. We’ll answer within 30 days.

Your consent

You agree to this policy when you tick the box as you create an account, and each time you sign in. You can withdraw that at any time by deleting your account, which stops all collection. Signing out stops everything but crash reports on that phone.

Changes

When this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date at the top. The app always links to the current version.
